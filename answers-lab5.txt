Q1. Do we have to add additional code to preserve the I/O-privilege (IOPL) flag across context switches? Why or why not?

A1. No. When env_create() sets the IOPL bits in the new environment’s trapframe (env->env_tf.tf_eflags), those bits become part of the saved EFLAGS value. The JOS context-switch mechanism—specifically env_pop_tf() and the corresponding save operation—always restores and preserves the entire trapframe, including EFLAGS, via the iret instruction. Consequently:

1. Upon entering the file system environment, iret reloads EFLAGS (with IOPL set) from env_tf, granting the environment its I/O privilege.
2. Upon leaving that environment, the kernel saves the current EFLAGS (including any modified IOPL bits) back into env_tf before switching to the next environment.

Because JOS unconditionally saves and restores the full EFLAGS register on every context switch, no additional code is required to maintain the IOPL setting. As long as tf_eflags is not overwritten elsewhere, the I/O-privilege flag persists correctly.
